Ch. 01 The throughline

Research that ships.

My work started with a question in my undergraduate bioinformatics lab: could machine learning actually change outcomes in medicine? That curiosity turned into a full-fledged passion for exploring this in depth. My research in computational biology taught me how in-silico data research could identify genetic matter that signalled the diagnosis of osteoblastic metastasis in prostate cancer patients.

Eventually, my passion for analyzing data and applying algorithms to it led me to a computer vision project in Siemens that won the Best Paper Award, at IEEE ICACCS and then to applying privacy-preserving algorithms to EHR data that predicts ICU mortality without ever moving a patient's data.

But research only matters when it runs. So I learned to ship: building agentic LLM systems inside Siemens' industrial software, designing the serving stacks and evaluation loops that prove a model works under real load. Today, at Carnegie Mellon and Qualcomm, I live in that seam between the frontier and production.

  • From 0 → 1 I prototype fast, then harden into reliable, observable systems.
  • Evidence-driven Offline + online evaluation, A/B tests, drift dashboards.
  • High-stakes domains Especially healthcare, where correctness and trust matter.
